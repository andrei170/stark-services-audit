Part one showed you who is advertising. This is what they are actually doing.

Eleven firms, and once you read all their advertising together the pattern is unmistakable. Seven things nearly every one of them does the same way, and four of those cost nothing to copy.

Then the part that matters: which of it we would do, which we would not, and what we would open on instead.

What They All Do, And What It Costs To Match
Video, every single time
All eleven use video, from six seconds to seventy-nine. Not one static image in the whole set.
NEEDED
Multiple variants per firm
4L runs six, Double R four, Texas Homestead four, 3T three. One ad is not a campaign.
NEEDED
Facebook lead forms, not their website
Nearly all send to fb.me rather than their own site. Only 3T sends traffic to its own domain.
CHOICE
The owner, by name
Texas Homestead is "Justin", Hydromulching is "Caleb". First person, phone number in the copy.
EASY
Insured and licensed, stated
Almost all of them say it explicitly. It is the standard reassurance in this trade.
EASY
A deadline
Texas Homestead runs "Get a free on-site estimate before September 30th" in the headline.
EASY
Reviews in the headline
Get-R-Done leads with "100+ Google Reviews". Nobody else does.
EASY

Four of those seven are free. Putting your own name in the copy, saying you are insured, running a dated offer and leading with your reviews cost nothing but the decision to do them. The two marked NEEDED are the ones that cost real money and time, and they are not optional — every firm in this market is on video, and the one that has lasted longest runs six cuts of the same message.

The Two Openings Nobody Is Taking
Where we would put your money

Do not open on land clearing. Eleven firms are fighting over it, two are competing on price, and one has an eight-month head start with six creative variants. It is the worst ground in this market to walk onto cold.

Open on pads and site prep instead. You told us pads are fast, high margin and decided quickly. Not one of eleven advertisers is bidding for them. That is the definition of an opening — a service you already make good money on, with a customer nobody else is talking to.

Drainage is the second one, and it belongs to the autumn. Nobody is advertising prevention, and the week after a storm the searches spike for whoever is already running.

The One Advert We Would Copy
Heaven to Earth Services — the strongest thing in the entire set

They tell the whole story of one job. A doctor moving from California to thirty acres in Smith County. An acre thick with brush, two more covered in years of deadfall. Five days of work. And then they publish what it cost: $12,500.

That is not a cheap-price advert, it is a credibility advert, and the difference matters enormously. Anybody who reads it knows what they are buying and roughly what it costs — so the people who ring have already accepted the number.

It filters out the price shoppers before they reach your phone. Cross Creek is doing the opposite with "$1,500 a day" and will spend the next year fielding calls from people who only heard the number.

We would build your first advert exactly this way. One real job you have done, told properly, with the price in it. You have better stories than they do.

The Order
  • Fix the flat $100 conversion value first. It costs nothing, and until it changes both Google and Meta will optimise towards your smallest and easiest jobs no matter what the adverts say.
  • Pads and site prep, told as one real job with the price in it. Video, and more than one cut.
  • The four free things — your name, insured and licensed, a dated offer, your reviews in the headline.
  • A Meta pixel. Exactly one firm in this entire market has one, so nobody else can tell which of their ads produces work.
Then
  • Drainage into the autumn, sold on prevention while the ground is still workable
  • Land clearing third, once something is working, and never on price
  • Commercial and agricultural site work, which nobody in this market is speaking to at all
  • Own Anderson County before Bronco does — nobody local advertises, so it is the cheapest ground you will ever buy

Everybody is selling clearing. Nobody is selling pads.

You already make better money on the work nobody is advertising for. That is where we would start, and the reason is not clever — it is just that the ground is empty.
