First — What We Actually Found, And It Is Better Than Most
Somebody has done real work here and it should be said plainly
  • Google Tag Manager is properly installed (container GTM-PGDXJN2W) and firing on the site.
  • A Google Ads account is connected through it — AW-17972349451, wired as a base tag rather than bodged into the theme.
  • Conversion Linker is switched on. This is the tag that stops Google losing track of a click between the ad and the enquiry. Most accounts we look at have it missing, and it quietly costs them conversions.
  • Call conversion tracking is configured against your real number, (903) 480-0158. For a business where the phone is how work actually starts, that is the right call and most contractors do not have it.
  • URL passthrough is enabled, which protects tracking when cookies are restricted.

This is not a broken account. It is a competently built one that is measuring the wrong thing — which for a business your shape is the more expensive of the two problems.

Finding 1 — Every Lead Reports As $100. All Of Them. Identical.
Read directly from your live Google Tag Manager container
Tag Google Ads Conversion (__awct) Account AW-17972349451 Value "100" Currency USD Meaning every single conversion is reported as worth $100

Here is why that matters more for you than for almost anyone else. Google's bidding does what you tell it to do. If every conversion is worth $100, then a tyre-kicker asking about a driveway and a landowner planning a month-long site development are, as far as Google is concerned, exactly the same event.

So it optimises for the cheapest possible conversions — because if they are all worth $100, the cheap ones are obviously better value. It will systematically find you more small enquiries and fewer big ones, and it will look like it is doing a good job while it does it, because the cost-per-conversion number will be falling.

What you want more of month-long development projects What this setup rewards whatever converts cheapest

You told our team you do 4 jobs a month and want 6. That is not a volume problem. Two more projects a month is a handful of extra genuine enquiries, not a flood. The entire game for you is which enquiries arrive, not how many — and the one number in your container that could tell Google that is currently switched off in the flattest possible way.

Finding 2 — The Form Conversion Depends On A Page We Could Not Find
Please verify this one yourself — we may be wrong, and it is worth two minutes
Conversion fires when page path starts with /thank-you /thank-you no response — same as a URL we invented to test In your sitemap not listed (7 pages, none of them a thank-you page)

Your web conversion is set to fire on a thank-you page. That is the standard way to do it and it is the right approach. But we could not reach that page — it behaved exactly the same as a deliberately fake URL we requested as a control, and it does not appear among the seven pages in your sitemap.

If that page genuinely is not there, or your form does not redirect to it — if it shows a message on the same page instead — then no form enquiry has ever registered as a conversion. Only phone calls would be counting. Your account would look worse than it is, and the bidding would be learning from half the picture.

How to check: fill in your own contact form and watch the address bar. If the URL changes to something with “thank-you” in it, we are wrong and everything is fine. If it stays on the contact page, this is the most valuable line in this document.

We are flagging this to verify, not asserting it. A thank-you page can be set to noindex and excluded from a sitemap, and a server can refuse our request for reasons that have nothing to do with whether the page exists.

Finding 3 — Your Analytics And Your Ads Live In Two Separate Systems
Corrected after a second pass — you have more here than a first look shows
Google Analytics 4 present — G-JY81LTQ9T0 Loaded via Google tag GT-M34XRWXK Google Ads AW-17972349451, configured separately in GTM Meta / Microsoft not present

You do have analytics, and our first pass missed it. It does not appear as a normal GA4 tag — it is routed through a Google tag, GT-M34XRWXK, which forwards to GA4 property G-JY81LTQ9T0. That is a legitimate and current way to set it up. Credit where it is due: this is more measurement than most contractors have.

What is worth confirming is that the two halves are talking to each other. Your analytics arrives through the Google tag; your ad conversions are built separately inside Tag Manager. Both work on their own. Whether GA4 is linked to the ads account — which is how audiences and quality signals travel between them — is set inside the accounts and cannot be seen from outside, so we are asking rather than asserting.

The reason it matters for you specifically: the fix for the $100 problem in Finding 1 is to send Google something that distinguishes a mulching enquiry from a barndominium. GA4 is the natural place that signal comes from. If the link is already in place, that work is far shorter than it would otherwise be.

Finding 4 — Enhanced Conversions Are Switched Off
One checkbox, free, in the tag that is already installed
Enhanced conversions disabled Cost to enable nothing Change to your site none

Enhanced conversions let Google match up enquiries it would otherwise lose — the ones where the browser blocked the cookie, or the person clicked on their phone and enquired on their laptop later. Without it a share of your real conversions simply never get counted, which makes the account look more expensive than it is and gives the bidding less to learn from.

This is the easiest fix in this document. It touches nothing you asked us not to touch.

Finding 5 — One Conversion Action Cannot Tell Your Jobs Apart
Eight services, one conversion, no way to separate them
Services you list 8 Conversion actions 1 web + 1 call Result underbrush mulching and a custom home are the same event

Your own services page lists concrete, custom homes and barndominiums, dirt work and pads, fencing and driveways, land clearing and ponds, metal buildings and shops, roofing, and underbrush mulching. Those are wildly different jobs at wildly different values, and every one of them currently reports as the same $100 event.

Your own services page makes the point better than we can: “clearing land leads to grading. Grading leads to a pad. A pad leads to concrete. Concrete leads to a shop, a barndominium, or a home build.” That is a ladder — and the advertising currently cannot tell which rung an enquiry came in on.

What we would do instead: separate the enquiry types, give each a value that reflects what that job is actually worth to you, and let the bidding chase the top of the ladder rather than the bottom of it. That is a change inside the ad account and the tag manager. Your website and your rankings are not involved.

Finding 6 — Four To Six Is A Qualification Problem, Not A Traffic Problem
Your stated goal, and what it actually implies
Now 4 jobs a month Goal 6 jobs a month Gap 2 projects — a 50% increase in revenue, from a handful of enquiries

This is a much better position than most people we speak to are in. You are not trying to fill a call centre. You need two more of the right conversations a month, from a business that already wins the work when it gets in front of it.

Which means throwing more traffic at it is the wrong instinct. More volume on a $100 flat conversion value gets you more of what is already cheapest — smaller jobs, more quoting, more of your time spent on work you did not want. The lever is qualification: fewer enquiries, better ones, and a bidding system that has been told which is which.

The honest caveat: we have not seen inside your ad account. We do not know your spend, your current cost per enquiry, how many of those enquiries turn into projects, or what an average project is worth to you. Everything above is read from your tracking setup, not from your results. Bring those four numbers to the call and we can be far more specific.

Fix First — None Of This Touches Your Website Or Your SEO
  • Test your own contact form and watch whether the URL changes to a thank-you page
  • Switch enhanced conversions on — one checkbox, free, no site change
  • Confirm GA4 (G-JY81LTQ9T0) is linked to the ads account — that link is how quality signals travel
  • Stop reporting every lead as $100 and give the different enquiry types different values
Build Next
  • Separate conversion actions per service, so a mulching enquiry and a barndominium enquiry stop looking identical
  • A qualifying step on the enquiry — acreage, timeline and scope — so the bidding learns from the right ones
  • A dedicated campaign landing page that ads point to, leaving your existing website exactly as it is
  • Then move to value-based bidding, once there are real values flowing in to bid on

You do not need more leads. You need Google to stop being told they are all the same.
