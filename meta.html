The first version of this document checked RLM, KEA and Boone & Boone and found nothing. That was correct and it was the wrong question — those are commercial general contractors who win work off bid lists, and they will never advertise on Facebook.

Finding 1 — One Ad Has Been Running For Eight Months
4L Land Management, same copy, six different video cuts
Started 20 Jan 2026 video 0:06 Started 21 Jan 2026 video 0:19 Started 12 Feb 2026 video 0:34 Started 12 Feb 2026 video 0:50 Started 08 Jul 2026 video 0:15 Started 08 Jul 2026 video 0:46 Identical ad copy on all six.

Nobody runs the same advert for eight months unless it is making money. That is the single most useful fact in this document, because it tells you the message works before you spend a pound finding out.

And look at what they are actually doing. One piece of copy, six different video cuts from six seconds to fifty. They are not testing messages, they are testing lengths — which means they found the message that works and are now finding the format that delivers it cheapest.

That is the creative volume conversation we had about Google, happening in front of you on Meta. Six variants from one firm. You would need the same.

Finding 2 — The Message Is The Same Everywhere
Opening lines, quoted from the live ads
4L: "Overgrown brush? Trees too thick to cut? Unusable land wasting your space and money?" 3T: "Overgrown Property? We Can Clear It." Bronco: "Got overgrown acres in East Texas? Let's turn them back into usable land." Double R: "Overgrown brush taking over your land?" Heaven: "Land that used to be usable is disappearing under brush."

Every one of them opens on the same idea: you own land you cannot use. Not a service, not a price, not the equipment. A problem the landowner already feels every time he looks out of the window.

And the payoff is always the same too. Homesites. Pasture. Fence lines. Food plots. Shooting lanes. Being able to walk your own property again. They are not selling mulching, they are selling the land back.

This is a solved problem and you do not have to solve it again. Five separate firms have converged on the same hook, which is about as strong a signal as advertising ever gives you.

Finding 3 — Two Of Them Are Publishing Prices
Quoted from the live ads
Cross Creek Farms: "Land clearing for $1,500 a day? Seriously." "You don't need contractor prices to clear your land." Heaven to Earth Services: "Our crew was there for 5 days. The full project came to $12,500."

Cross Creek is competing on being the cheap option and saying so in the advert. That sets a number in the head of every landowner in East Texas who sees it, and you will meet that number in your own quotes whether you advertise or not.

Heaven to Earth is doing something far more interesting. They tell the whole story of one job — a doctor moving from California to thirty acres in Smith County, an acre of brush, two acres of deadfall, five days of work — and then they publish what it cost. $12,500.

That is not a cheap-price advert. It is a credibility advert. Anybody who reads it knows exactly what they are buying and roughly what it will cost, which filters out the tyre kickers before they ever call. It is the best-written advert in this whole set and it is the one we would build your first campaign against.

Finding 4 — Nobody Is Advertising What You Actually Do Best
The gaps, and they are the valuable part of this document

Every single one of these eleven firms is selling the same thing: clearing overgrown land for a landowner. That is one of your services. It is not the most profitable one and it is not the one you called mailbox money.

  • Not one advertises pads for modular or mobile homes. You told us those are fast, high margin and decided quickly. Nobody in this market is competing for them on Meta.
  • Not one advertises site preparation for a new build — getting in front of the customer before the builder is chosen.
  • Not one advertises drainage. Nobody is selling prevention, and nobody is positioned for the week after a storm.
  • Not one mentions commercial or agricultural site work. They are all talking to homeowners with acreage.
  • Not one of them is in Anderson County. The closest are Tyler and Smith County, which is a different market with different ground.

So the picture is not "everyone is doing it and you are behind". It is that eleven firms are crowded onto one service in counties next to yours, and the services you make the most money on are uncontested.

Finding 5 — They Are Buying Into The Season You Write Off

You told us autumn is slow and that November and December are dead months. Nine of these eleven firms started advertising in August and September.

That does not prove they are right and you are wrong. They may be wasting money, and the ad library cannot tell us whether any of it is working. But nine firms independently deciding to spend money going into your slow season is worth knowing about, and it is exactly the question the seasonality document was written to answer.

The honest read: the demand does not vanish in the autumn, it changes shape. Somebody planning a spring homesite is looking in October. Whoever is visible then gets the enquiry.

What Comes Next
These were the three questions. Part two is what your answers produced.

We knew what we would run and in what order. It comes straight out of the four findings above.

What we would not do was put it in writing before asking you three questions, because the answers change the plan enough that guessing would have been irresponsible. You have since answered them, and Part Two is the plan those answers produced.

Which service do you actually want more of? The gap in this market is in pads, site prep, drainage and commercial work. Those are four different campaigns with four different customers, and we are not going to pick for you.

How much of your crew capacity is genuinely spare, and when? Advertising into a diary that is already full is the fastest way to waste money and annoy customers, and your year has a very particular shape.

What is a job actually worth to you, by type? Everything in your account currently reports at a flat $100. Until that changes, both Google and Meta will optimise towards your smallest and easiest work no matter what we write in the ads.

Bring rough answers to Friday and you will leave the call with the plan. It is twenty minutes of conversation and it is worth more than anything we could have guessed at here.

We have also pulled apart exactly what these eleven firms do in their advertising — the formats, the offers, the proof they lead with and what it would cost you to match it. That is written up and we will go through it with you on the call.

Eleven firms, one service, and none of them in your county.

They are all selling land clearing to landowners in Tyler and Smith County. Nobody is advertising pads, site prep, drainage or commercial work — which is most of what you actually make money on.

Which of those four to open on is the whole conversation on Friday.
