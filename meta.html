What we could not verify is active ad creative. You can check that yourself in about sixty seconds, and the links to do it are at the bottom of this page. We would rather hand you the tool than tell you what we think is in it.

Which Means One Of Two Things
And we are not going to pretend we know which

Either the lane is genuinely empty and there is ground to take — four established commercial contractors have all overlooked the same channel.

Or Meta simply does not work for commercial construction in East Texas, four experienced firms have all worked that out independently, and they are right not to bother.

Our honest reading is that it is mostly the second, with a real exception. Nobody scrolling Facebook commissions a $600,000 build. Those buyers are on bid lists and in relationships, not in a feed.

But that is only true of the commercial half of your business.

Where Meta Would Genuinely Work For You
And it is the half of your business nobody else is competing for
  • Your "mailbox money" work. Modular home pads and quick turnarounds are close to impulse purchases, they are visual, and they are decided by homeowners rather than procurement. That is exactly what Meta sells well.
  • Showing finished work. Construction is among the most visual trades there is. Before-and-after content performs on Facebook in a way it never will on a search results page — and you have 80% of the work done in-house, so the photographs are yours to take.
  • Being known in Anderson County. Palestine is small enough that sustained local presence compounds. People recognise the name before they need you, and in a town this size that is worth more than in a city.
  • Your background. An IT and cybersecurity man running a construction company is an unusual story. Search cannot tell a story. Meta can. None of RLM, KEA or Boone & Boone has anything comparable to say.
The Off-Season Argument — This Is The Real Case For Meta
You asked about a winter plan. This is where the two questions meet.

Search volume falls in winter. Scrolling does not. When people stop typing "commercial contractor near me" because nothing is getting built until spring, your Google campaigns have less to bid on — and the cost per lead on what remains tends to rise.

Meta behaves differently. The audience is still there, the inventory is still there, and demand from other advertisers in a small East Texas market does not spike the way it does in retail. Winter is when Meta is comparatively cheapest and Google is comparatively dearest.

So the sensible shape is not "Google or Meta". It is Google in season, Meta out of it — with the budget shifting between them rather than sitting idle or being switched off.

Winter is also when brand-building costs least and matters most, because the projects being decided in January and February are the ones that get built in spring. That is the case for Meta, and it is a seasonal one rather than a channel one.

We will bring the actual on-season and off-season cost-per-lead figures to the next call, as agreed — that is the number that decides how much moves and when.

What We Would Not Do
Being clear about the limits

We would not put your commercial budget into Meta. The $350k–$600k projects you told us you want are not won there, and any agency promising otherwise is selling.

We would not start with Meta. Your Google account already has seven conversion tags installed and an ads account with history. Fixing the conversion values there produces a result faster and more cheaply than building a new channel from nothing.

And we would not run Meta without the pixel installed first. Which is a ten-minute job, and worth doing now regardless — because a pixel installed today starts building an audience you can advertise to in three months. It costs nothing to switch on and it cannot be backdated.
