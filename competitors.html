The Honest Thing First
Because it changes what advertising is actually for

You will not buy a $350,000 commercial project off Google Ads. That work is won on bids, relationships and being on somebody's shortlist before the project exists. No advertising platform sells it directly, and anyone telling you otherwise is selling.

What advertising does is get you invited. It puts you in front of the people who compile those shortlists, and it captures the search demand that does exist — which in commercial construction is smaller, more specific, and far less contested than the residential side.

Everything below is written on that basis.

Your Small-Job Competition Is A Different Set Entirely
And this is where advertising works immediately

Locally in Palestine and Anderson County: Caprock Roofing Construction (established 2021) and Lovelady Remodeling (25 years in East Texas), among a thin field of general contractors listed on the directories.

This is a small market and that cuts both ways. Fewer competitors bidding against you on search, but also less volume to capture. The upside is that clicks in a market this size are cheap — which is exactly why the small-job campaign funds itself while the commercial one builds.

Your "mailbox money" work sits here — modular home pads and quick turnarounds. That is the most immediately winnable ground you have.

The whole advantage is currently switched off by one number. Every conversion reports $100, so Google cannot tell a $600,000 commercial enquiry from an $8,000 repair — and buys whichever click is cheapest.

Fix that and you are the only contractor in East Texas bidding on commercial terms who can tell which enquiries are worth having. That is a real, defensible edge, and it does not depend on being older or bigger than RLM.

How To Position Against Them
Three things they cannot easily copy
  • 80% of the work in-house. Most commercial GCs at $350k–$600k subcontract heavily. Direct control of quality is a genuine differentiator and it is checkable.
  • You are in Anderson County and they are not. Local presence matters on projects where somebody wants the contractor thirty minutes away rather than two hours.
  • Your IT and cybersecurity background. A general contractor who came from technology is an unusual story, and it lands with exactly the kind of commercial client who cares about documentation, systems and process. None of the four firms above can say it.

What not to do: compete on being established, on size, or on how long you have been trading. Those are RLM's ground and you will lose on all three.

What We Would Do About It
  • Fix the conversion values first — nothing else works until Google knows what a lead is worth
  • Take the small-job search in Anderson County, where the field is thin and clicks are cheap
  • Run a narrow commercial campaign on specific high-intent terms rather than broad construction keywords
  • Lead the commercial copy on in-house delivery and local presence, not on experience
  • Build the authority angle around your background — it is the one thing none of them has

You cannot out-age RLM. You can out-measure all four of them.

Seven conversion tags already firing, and every one saying $100. That is the whole opportunity.
