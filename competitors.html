Finding 1 — You Are The Best-Equipped Firm Here, And The Only One Not Using It
This is the whole point of the document

Seven working conversion tags. Analytics. A working enquiry form. Across every firm in both tables, nobody else has that combination. Two of your Palestine competitors do not have a form at all. Most of the Meta advertisers are measuring nothing.

You built the machine and never switched it on. They switched theirs on without building one.

The one thing still missing is the conversion value. Everything is reporting at a flat $100, which means a pad job and a twenty-acre clear look identical to the bidding algorithm. Fix that and you are the only contractor in East Texas who can tell Google and Meta which jobs are worth chasing.

Finding 2 — Bronco Is The One To Watch
The only firm running Google and Meta together
Google Ads tag present Meta ads live 2, since 1 August 2026 Analytics present Meta pixel none Enquiry form none found

Bronco is doing what we would do, one county over. Both channels, running now, and their Facebook copy is the best-written thing in the whole market — long-form, local, leading with “before you hire anyone to clear your land, read this” and built entirely around being the firm you might run into at the feed store.

They are also making two mistakes. No Meta pixel, so they cannot tell which ads produce work. And no enquiry form on the website, so their Google traffic has nowhere to go but the phone.

They are in Tyler and you are in Palestine. Right now that is a different market. If they start bidding on Anderson County searches, it stops being one.

Finding 3 — The Local Firms Are Not Coming For You Yet

CJ Enterprises has been in Anderson County for over twenty years and has no analytics, no form and no advertising. They are winning work the way everybody in this trade always has — word of mouth, the sign on the truck, and being known.

That is not a criticism of them. It is the shape of the opportunity. The local market is entirely unadvertised, which means the cost of being the first voice in it is as low as it will ever be.

Mud Rock is the one exception worth noting — their website leans very heavily on customer reviews. No advertising, but they clearly understand that proof is what sells this work.

Finding 4 — Everybody Is Selling The Same Service
What all eleven Meta advertisers lead with
Land clearing / forestry mulching 11 of 11 Pads for modular or mobile homes 0 of 11 Site preparation for a new build 0 of 11 Drainage 0 of 11 Commercial or agricultural site work 0 of 11

Every firm advertising in East Texas is selling the same thing to the same person: clearing overgrown land for somebody who owns acreage.

Nobody is advertising the work you called mailbox money. Pads are fast, high margin and decided quickly, and not one of eleven firms is competing for them. The same is true of site prep before the builder is chosen, and of drainage.

So the market is not saturated. It is crowded onto one service and empty everywhere else. The full ad-by-ad breakdown, including the copy that has been running for eight months, is in the Meta analysis.

What We Would Do About It
  • Fix the flat $100 conversion value first. It costs nothing and without it every campaign optimises towards your smallest jobs.
  • Advertise pads and site prep, not land clearing. Eleven firms are fighting over clearing and two are competing on price. Nobody is bidding for the work you make most money on.
  • Own Anderson County before Bronco does. Nobody local is advertising, so the cost of being first is the lowest it will ever be.
Then
  • Add a Meta pixel — only one firm in this entire market has one
  • Video for Meta, because every single competitor uses it and none of them uses stills
  • Land clearing second, once something is working, and never on price

You are better equipped than every firm in this document.

Seven conversion tags, analytics and a working form, in a county where nobody advertises at all. The only thing you are missing is being in the market.
