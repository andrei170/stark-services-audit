5,500 Live Ads For Land Clearing. 44 For Pad Prep. Zero For Barndominium Pads.

0
live Meta ads in the US for "barndominium pad" or "barndo pad"

Parts 1 and 2 showed East Texas crowding onto clearing. So we searched the whole country. It is the same everywhere — and the pad lane is still almost empty.

We read 216 live adverts across ten searches. Clearing is a crowded auction. Pads are a handful of small firms. Barndominium pads — the thing half of rural Texas is building — nobody is advertising at all.

Finding 1 — Everyone Is Launching Right Now
When the 216 live adverts we read started running: Mar 2026 11 Apr 11 May 16 Jun 16 Jul 2026 22 Aug 28 Sep (first 17 days) 36
The fall push is happening this month

More adverts started in the first half of September than in any full month before it. McGinn Property Services in New York says it out loud: "Fall is closing in fast, and once the ground freezes, your excavation window closes with it... Our fall schedule is filling fast."

East Texas does not freeze the way New York does — which is exactly why fall and winter are your advantage. Part 1 showed competitors buying into the season you write off. Nationally, they are doing it now.

Finding 2 — The Few Firms Selling Pads, And What They Say
Clear Flow Services, East Tennessee — the best pad advert we found
"Ready to build? Clear Flow clears lots and preps pads for new home construction across Loudon, Knox, Roane, Anderson, Morgan & Monroe counties." "Minimum projects start at $3,000 for site work. That includes the basics—tree and brush removal, grading for a building pad, erosion control, and debris hauling."

Two things worth copying: it names the exact counties, and it publishes a starting price with what it includes. Its longer version tells a story — a homeowner who "hired three different crews in eight months" while "the builder had delayed framing for six weeks" — which is the real pain of a pad buyer: the build is waiting on the dirt.

Stepan Property and Development, Alabama — one team for the whole site
"Land clearing, excavation, grading, building pads, drainage, driveways, demolition, hauling, utilities, and more — all managed under one roof." "One Team. Your Entire Property Project."

Five live adverts, two running multiple versions. The offer is not "clearing" — it is "raw land to a build-ready site, one contractor". That is the Stark pitch.

In Texas — only a handful mention pads at all
Daniel Dean Land Clearing & Dirt Work (Magnolia, TX): "Whether you're clearing raw land, building a house pad, cutting a driveway... handles the whole job." "✅ House Pads & Commercial Building Pads" Rockin' A Timber (East Texas): "We turn overgrown East Texas properties into build-ready land" Montgomery Construction LLC (Montgomery County, TX): "Need driveway repairs, pad prep, or drainage fixed before the next downpour?"

All three list pads as one bullet among many. Nobody in Texas leads with the pad.

Finding 3 — Land Sellers Are Already Advertising Pads As A Selling Point
Tyler, TX land listing: "3.199 UNRESTRICTED Acres for Sale — $120,000" "🚧 50' × 150' building pad already prepared" Lake Livingston lot listing: "cleared and clay house pad installed. Ready to set your slab forms and start building." Texas Country Land Listings (Floresville, TX): "3.4–5.5 acre lots... rural water, power, and pad sites."
This is a second customer you are not talking to

People selling rural land in Texas are putting "building pad already prepared" in the headline of their listing, because a build-ready lot sells faster. Land sellers, developers and rural realtors are pad buyers too — and no contractor we found is advertising to them.

Finding 4 — Watch Out For Template Adverts
Two firms, 700 miles apart, running the same advert word for word
Butler Contracting LLC (Greater Huntsville, AL) and All Angles Construction (greater Houston, TX): "What is the real cost of getting your land cleared?" "Are you hearing extravagant prices just to take some brush out of your backyard or prep a site for a new build?" "...we aren't a massive corporation with sky-high overhead. We are a local, owner-operated business..."

Identical copy, only the town swapped — almost certainly the same agency template. It is exactly what we will not do for you. Your advert should only work for Stark, because it talks about your pads, your county and your jobs.

Finding 6 — The Adverts That Have Lasted
Longevity is the closest public sign that an advert is paying for itself
  • McDaniel's Grading & Hauling — 7 live image adverts, all running since 31 March 2023. Three and a half years without switching them off.
  • Land Clearing & Forestry Mulching Pros — running since May 2025, with multiple versions. It is a lead seller: "Fill out the form to get up to 3 quotes" — the kind of middleman you do not want your customers going through.
  • Stepan Property and Development — newer (July 2026), but already testing multiple versions of its "one team" adverts.
What We Would Run For Stark
  • Lead with the pad, not the clearing. "Building a barndo in Anderson County? Your build starts with the pad." Nobody in the country is running it.
  • Publish a starting price with what it includes — the Clear Flow move. It filters out tyre-kickers before they ring.
  • Tell the "build is waiting on the dirt" story — the builder delayed, the bank waiting — because that is why pad buyers hire fast.
  • A second campaign aimed at land sellers and rural realtors — "a lot with a pad sells faster" — the customer nobody is advertising to.
  • Launch now, into fall and winter, while the national push is already under way and your ground stays workable.
  • Keep clearing as the supporting service, not the headline — that auction has 5,500 adverts in it.
What We Could Not Check
Marked honestly rather than guessed
  • Spend, results or profit for any advertiser — the library shows what is running, not what it earns.
  • The text of image-only adverts, such as McDaniel's.
  • Every relevant advert. The library loads a sample per search; we read 216 unique adverts across ten searches, and some searches hit rate limits.
  • Where every advertiser is based — we only state a location when the advert itself says it.

Everybody is selling clearing. Nobody is selling the barndo pad.

Part 2 found the gap in East Texas. Part 3 found it is empty across the whole country.
