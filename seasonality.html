Two dead months, three slow ones, and a drought risk in high summer. You also told us it got dry enough this year that you did not want to be out clearing at all — fire risk. So the weather closes your window from both ends.

The Question Everyone Gets Wrong
Read this bit twice. It is the whole argument.
November and December are dead for doing the work.
They are not dead for winning it.

Nobody clears land in December. But people decide in December what they are clearing in March.

A commercial project that breaks ground in spring was budgeted and bid over the winter. The decision happens months before the machinery moves.

So the dead months are not dead demand. They are delayed demand. The enquiry still happens; the job just starts later.

And Here Is The Part That Favours You
What happens to advertising costs when everyone else stops

Your competitors think the same way you do. Work slows, so they pull back or switch off.

When advertisers leave an auction, the ones who stay pay less. Fewer bidders on the same searches means a lower cost per click and a lower cost per lead.

So the months when the work is cheapest to win are the months everyone abandons.

That is a general pattern in seasonal trades, not a promise about your account. We do not yet have your month-by-month numbers — and we are not going to invent them. What we can say is that this is the shape to expect, and that the first winter is when you find out what your own numbers actually are.

Three Ways To Play It
Option A

Wait until spring

Set everything up now, launch in February. Nothing is spent over the dead months.

The case for it: no wasted budget, and you start when the phones would ring anyway.

The case against: you launch into the busiest, most expensive, most contested part of the year with a brand new account that has no data in it. Google's algorithm learns from history, and you would have none. The first six weeks would be the account working out what a good lead looks like — at peak prices.

Option B — our recommendation

Start small in the quiet months, scale in spring

Run a reduced budget through November and December. Not the full programme — enough to keep the account learning and collect the winter enquiries that do exist.

Why this is the sensible one:

1. The account arrives at spring already trained. By March it knows which enquiries convert, and value-based bidding has real data behind it. That is worth more than the budget you saved.

2. You find out your own seasonal numbers instead of guessing at them next year.

3. Winter clicks are cheaper, so the learning costs less than it would in May.

4. Spring work gets decided in winter. You are in front of those people while your competitors are not.

Option C

Full budget straight through

Run the whole programme regardless of season.

The case for it: maximum share of whatever winter demand exists, and the strongest possible position by March.

The case against: we would not recommend it and we would rather say so. Spending full budget into two months you have described as dead is how people conclude advertising does not work. The honest version is that it works, but not evenly across the year.

What We Would Actually Do, Month By Month
  • Sept–Oct — build and launch. Fix conversion values, get the account running while there is still work about
  • Nov–Dec — reduce spend, keep it live. Shift the message from "we can start now" to "let's plan your spring"
  • Jan–Feb — step spend back up. This is when spring budgets are set and bids go out
  • Mar onward — full budget into a trained account, at the start of your season rather than the middle

The one thing we would not do is switch off entirely and restart. An account that goes dark loses its learning, and you would pay to rebuild it in the most expensive month of the year.

What Changes In The Ads Themselves
Same budget, different message

In season — urgency. Availability, how soon you can start, capacity this month.

Out of season — planning. Spring bookings, getting a quote in before the rush, budgeting for next year's project.

It is the same customer. They are just at a different point in their thinking, and the ad should meet them there.

This is also where Meta earns its place — winter is when search volume falls but scrolling does not, and when brand-building costs least. That is covered properly in the Meta analysis.

You said November and December are dead months. You are right about the work.

But the jobs you build in March get decided in January. The question is whether you are visible when that happens.
